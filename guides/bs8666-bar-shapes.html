What the shape code means

BS 8666 (Scheduling, dimensioning, bending and cutting of steel reinforcement for concrete) assigns every bent bar a two-digit shape code. The code fixes the geometry: which dimensions are labelled A, B, C, D, E — and the formula that turns those dimensions into the total cutting length L measured along the bar.

The key idea: you add the outside dimensions, then subtract the bend deductions (the r and d terms in each formula). A bent bar is shorter than the sum of its legs, because the bend itself consumes less material than two square corners would.

Dimensions in parentheses are “free”. On a schedule, the dimension in brackets is the one allowed to absorb the bending tolerance — the fabricator holds the others exact.

Minimum bend radii (Table 2)

The bend deduction needs r, the minimum scheduling radius for the bar size:

Φ (mm)6810121620253240
r (mm)12162024327087112140

Up to 16 mm the rule is simply r = 2d; larger bars need proportionally bigger formers.

Worked example — stirrup, shape 51

A Φ10 link, A = 400, B = 400, hook C = 130 mm. From the table, r = 20 mm:

  1. Formula: L = 2(A + B + C) − 2.5r − 5d
  2. L = 2(400 + 400 + 130) − 2.5(20) − 5(10)
  3. L = 1860 − 50 − 50 = 1760 mm
Cutting length: 1.76 m per stirrup. At 0.616 kg/m, each stirrup weighs 1.08 kg — try it in the calculator above.
