The formula

A rebar is a steel cylinder, so its weight per metre is its cross-sectional area times the density of steel (7850 kg/m³):

w = 0.006165 × d² — unit weight in kg/m, with d the nominal bar diameter in mm.
Derived as (π/4) × d² × 7850 / 10⁶.

Multiply by the bar length for the weight of one bar, and by the number of bars for the total — divide by 1000 for tonnes.

Worked example

An order of 40 bars, Φ20, 12 m:

  1. Unit weight: 0.006165 × 20² = 2.466 kg/m
  2. One 12 m bar: 2.466 × 12 = 29.59 kg
  3. 40 bars: 29.59 × 40 = 1,183.7 kg ≈ 1.18 tonnes
Result: about 1.18 t of steel. At an indicative €720/t, that order costs roughly €850 — before cutting waste, which is why the cutting plan matters.

Why estimators care

  • Ordering: suppliers sell by the tonne — convert your cutting list to tonnage before requesting a quote.
  • Waste allowance: the 3–5% typically added for cutting waste is applied to this tonnage.
  • Checks: weighbridge tickets vs. calculated tonnage is the standard way to verify a delivery.
